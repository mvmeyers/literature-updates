### 2. Amygdala neurofeedback as an adjunct to residential dialectical behavior therapy for borderline personality disorder: a pilot randomized controlled trial

- **Authors:** Hofmanová M, Santangelo PS, Kleindienst N, Schmahl C, Paret C
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** BMC Psychiatry (Springer)
- **DOI:** 10.1186/s12888-026-08767-7
- **Google Scholar URL:** https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-026-08767-7

**Abstract:**

Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is characterized by affective instability and amygdala hyperreactivity. Although dialectical behavior therapy (DBT) reduces symptoms and amygdala-related abnormalities, many patients show only partial remission. Real-time functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) amygdala neurofeedback (amy-NF) directly targets hyperactive limbic responses and has shown promise in uncontrolled trials. The authors examined whether amy-NF adds benefit to inpatient DBT. Thirty-six mid-therapy people with BPD and clinically significant residual symptoms (BSL-23 > 1.5) were randomized to DBT plus amy-NF (n=23) or DBT alone (treatment as usual, n=13). Ecological momentary assessment (EMA) of daily-life affective instability served as the primary outcome; secondary outcomes included self-reports, amygdala responses during two fMRI tasks, and resting-state fMRI, assessed pre-, post-, 3-, and 6-months. Participants successfully downregulated amygdala activity during neurofeedback. EMA showed improvements in negative affect, inner tension, and affective instability over time in both groups, without consistent superiority of amy-NF; self-reports likewise showed no additional benefit, and BSL-23 scores increased after discharge in both groups. Amy-NF was associated with transient pre-post reductions in amygdala activity during the emotion regulation task and more stable amygdala reactivity over time than TAU in a faces task, plus transient increases in resting-state amygdala connectivity with precuneus and dorsomedial frontal regions. The authors conclude amygdala neurofeedback is feasible and reduces amygdala activity in BPD but did not yield additional clinical benefit alongside intensive residential DBT, though the small sample may have limited power to detect such effects.

**Abstract:**

### 10. Elevated borderline features in bipolar disorder: Clinical characteristics and symptom course in South Korea

- **Authors:** Jang E, Shin YE, Choi MJ, Lee H, Myung W, Ha TH, Ahn YM, Ha K, Hong KS, Baek JH
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Journal of Affective Disorders (Elsevier)
- **DOI:** 10.1016/j.jad.2026.122639
- **Google Scholar URL:** https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S016503272601493X

**Abstract:**

Elevated borderline features (EBF) in bipolar disorder (BD) are associated with a more severe illness course, but prospective evidence remains limited, particularly in Asian populations. The authors examined the clinical correlates and 6-month depressive symptom course of EBF in Korean patients with BD. Participants were 520 patients with BD-I or BD-II from three South Korea tertiary hospitals. EBF was identified using the self-report Personality Assessment Inventory-Borderline Features Scale (score ≥ 38). Clinical characteristics were compared between EBF and non-EBF groups after adjusting for demographic and clinical covariates, and 6-month depressive symptom trajectories under naturalistic treatment were compared. Overall, 237 (45.5%) met the EBF criterion. After adjustment, EBF was associated with more depressive episodes, greater childhood trauma, lifetime self-harm, suicidal ideation, suicide attempts, violence, and several comorbidities. Depressive symptoms improved in both groups, but the EBF group maintained greater severity across follow-up (adjusted mean difference = 4.00, 95% CI = 1.83–6.18), with a nonsignificant EBF-by-time interaction. EBF was associated with lower odds of response (adjusted OR = 0.40, 95% CI = 0.18–0.91); remission did not differ. EBF was defined by a self-report screening measure rather than a structured diagnostic interview, and clinical correlates were examined cross-sectionally, so causal relationships cannot be inferred. Among Korean patients with BD, EBF was associated with greater illness burden and persistently greater depressive symptom severity, extending predominantly Western evidence to an Asian population and supporting assessing borderline features to guide risk evaluation and longitudinal monitoring.
