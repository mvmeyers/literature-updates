This article addresses supervision practices in transference-focused psychotherapy (TFP) for borderline personality disorder and personality dysfunction. ### 2. Undoing Self-Contempt in Borderline Personality Disorder: A Controlled Test of Change in Emotion Processing in Psychotherapy

- **Authors:** Kramer U, Grandjean L, Beuchat H, Kolly S
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Clinical Psychology & Psychotherapy (September–October 2026 issue)
- **DOI:** 10.1002/cpp.70335
- **Google Scholar URL:** https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/cpp.70335

**Abstract:**

This controlled study examined emotion processing mechanisms in psychotherapy for borderline personality disorder. Researchers hypothesized that reduced self-contempt during treatment would correlate with increased self-compassion and assertive anger. The investigation involved 79 participants across three groups: 27 individuals with BPD receiving Good Psychiatric Management (GPM), 23 receiving treatment as usual (TAU), and 29 healthy controls. All participants engaged in out-of-therapy two-chair dialogues addressing self-criticism. Assessment tools included video-rated Classification of Affective Meaning States and the Self-Contempt Rating Scale. Results demonstrated that the intensity of out-of-session observed self-contempt decreased in clients with BPD undergoing GPM, with a large effect. The GPM group showed increased access to self-compassion and assertive anger, distinguishing it from control and TAU groups. (PubMed abstract, via efetch.)

- **DOI:** 10.1097/jcp.0000000000002249
- **Google Scholar URL:** https://www.ovid.com/jnls/psychopharmacology/abstract/10.1097/jcp.0000000000002249

**Abstract:**

This naturalistic follow-up study compared pharmacological efficacy and tolerability of low-dose clozapine versus aripiprazole in borderline personality disorder treatment. Twenty-three patients received either clozapine (n=9) or aripiprazole (n=14), assessed at admission, discharge, and three-month follow-up across emotional dysregulation, dissociation, depression, impulsivity, and quality of life. Both medication groups demonstrated significant improvements in dissociation, depression, impulsivity, and quality of life at discharge and follow-up. Clozapine was associated with greater reductions in dissociative experiences at hospital discharge and in depressive symptoms at follow-up. Both treatments proved safe and well-tolerated, suggesting clozapine may offer advantages for severe cases with prominent dissociative or depressive features. (PubMed abstract, via efetch.)
