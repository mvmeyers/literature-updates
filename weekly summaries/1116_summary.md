### 1. Application of a Brief, Personality-Based Intervention for Borderline Personality Disorder for a Patient With Intersecting Identities: A BPD Compass Case Study

- **Authors:** Bowling WR, Southward MW, Burris JL, Sauer-Zavala S
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Journal of Clinical Psychology
- **DOI:** 10.1002/jclp.70245
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43002641/

**Abstract:**

Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is a heterogeneous condition characterized by emotion dysregulation, interpersonal dysfunction, impulsivity, identity disturbance, and elevated rates of co-occurrence with other mental health conditions. Criterion B of the Alternative Model of Personality Disorders (AMPD) conceptualizes BPD symptoms as manifestations of dimensional personality traits, particularly negative affectivity, antagonism, and disinhibition. BPD Compass (Cognitive-behavioral Modules for Personality Symptoms) is an 18-session modular cognitive-behavioral intervention designed to target these higher-order personality dimensions and their underlying maintenance mechanisms. This article presents a detailed case study illustrating the application of BPD Compass with a 25-year-old bisexual Appalachian woman diagnosed with BPD, persistent depressive disorder, binge eating disorder, and subclinical social anxiety disorder. Treatment integrated values clarification, cognitive flexibility, behavioral skills, and mindfulness strategies tailored to the patient's cultural and identity-related experiences. Throughout treatment, interventions were personalized to address the impact of Appalachian cultural norms, minority stress related to sexual identity, emotional avoidance, interpersonal mistrust, and impulsive coping behaviors. By posttreatment, the patient demonstrated substantial reductions in BPD symptom severity, with Zanarini Rating Scale scores decreasing from 23 to 6. She no longer met criteria for binge eating disorder or clinically significant social anxiety symptoms and reported meaningful improvements in emotion regulation, interpersonal functioning, and impulse control.

### 2. Impulsivity, compulsivity, and impulse control disorders in adults with elevated borderline personality disorder traits

- **Authors:** Avila L, Neelapu M, Boutouis S, Grant JE
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Journal of Behavioral Addictions
- **DOI:** 10.1556/2006.2026.00194
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43007360/

**Abstract:**

BACKGROUND AND AIMS: While impulsivity is a core feature of borderline personality disorder (BPD), rates of comorbid impulse control disorders in those with BPD are unclear. Moreover, despite the tendency for impulsivity and compulsivity to present together, compulsivity has been understudied in BPD.

METHODS: 289 participants responded to an online survey on Prolific and completed the following measures: McLean Screening Instrument for BPD, to identify those with elevated BPD traits; the Barratt Impulsiveness Scale-15 (BIS-15), to examine trait impulsivity; the Cambridge-Chicago Compulsivity Trait Scale (CHI-T), to assess compulsivity; and the Minnesota Impulse Disorders Interview, to screen for probable impulse control disorders.

RESULTS: 13.1% of the sample met criteria for elevated BPD traits (mean age = 33.24, SD = 11.79; 55.3% women). Those with elevated traits scored significantly higher on both trait impulsivity and compulsivity after controlling for age, sexual orientation, and comorbidities. The compulsivity elevation was primarily driven by the reward drive subscale. Participants with elevated traits were significantly more likely to screen positive for probable compulsive sexual behavior, but there were no significant differences in rates of other impulse control disorders (i.e., binge eating, compulsive buying, gambling, and trichotillomania).

DISCUSSION: Individuals with BPD may exhibit more compulsivity than expected, challenging the notion that BPD is solely an impulsive disorder. Compulsive sexual behavior was the one impulse control disorder more common in this group, though whether this reflects a distinctive feature of BPD or greater statistical power for a more prevalent disorder remains unclear.

CONCLUSIONS: Both impulsive and compulsive tendencies appear relevant to BPD and its comorbidities. Treatments addressing compulsivity, alongside the impulsivity and emotion dysregulation typically targeted, may warrant further study.

### 3. Screening for borderline personality disorder after self-harm episodes: validation of the self-rating KABOSS-BD, the borderline subscale of the Karolinska Affective and borderline symptoms scale

- **Authors:** Asellus P, Doering S, Renberg ES, Runeson B, Waern M
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** BMC Psychiatry
- **DOI:** 10.1111/acps.13166 (this DOI prefix/pattern does not match the stated journal/publisher — likely a source-metadata inconsistency; see Classification notes)
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43009864/

**Abstract:**

BACKGROUND: Borderline personality disorder (BPD) is overrepresented among individuals with deliberate self-harm, yet identification of patients requiring diagnostic evaluation remains challenging in emergency settings. Effective screening tools may facilitate earlier identification of symptoms, shorten diagnostic delay, and improve treatment planning. The present study sought to validate the borderline subscale of the Karolinska Affective and Borderline Symptoms Scale (KABOSS-BD) as a self-rating screening tool for BPD in patients presenting with self-harm, and to examine its correlation with clinical diagnosis.

METHODS: Data were analysed from 671 patients presenting with self-harm (with or without suicidal intention) at emergency departments in three Swedish cities. We investigated the internal validity of KABOSS-BD and its correlation with clinically diagnosed BPD (ICD-10 Emotionally Unstable Personality Disorder) and determined optimal cut-off scores for women and men for screening purposes.

RESULTS: The KABOSS-BD demonstrated good internal consistency (Cronbach's α = 0.87) and acceptable diagnostic accuracy in identifying patients with BPD, with an Area Under the Curve (AUC) of 0.73. The optimal cut-off score was 32, yielding sensitivity of 68% and specificity of 68%. Women consistently scored higher than men on the KABOSS-BD, and gender-specific cut-offs improved diagnostic accuracy. The scale performed optimally for women with a cut-off at 33, while for men, the scale performed better at a lower threshold of 26.

CONCLUSIONS: The KABOSS-BD demonstrates good internal consistency and acceptable diagnostic accuracy for BPD screening in both women and men who present with suicide attempts and non-suicidal self-harm. The scale's brevity and ease of administration make it suitable for emergency settings, potentially reducing diagnostic delay and improving treatment allocation for this high-risk population.

### 4. An Ecological Model of Recognition: Diagnosis as a Relational Process Among Survivors of Childhood Sexual Abuse

- **Authors:** Klein M, Farchi M, Meir N, Peled-Avram M
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Journal of Interpersonal Violence
- **DOI:** 10.1177/08862605261490536
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010712/

**Abstract:**

While the diagnostic overlap between complex post-traumatic stress disorder (CPTSD) and borderline personality disorder (BPD) has received increasing scholarly attention, there remains a limited understanding of how adult women survivors of childhood sexual abuse (CSA) interpret psychiatric diagnoses within the relational contexts that shape their self-recognition and meaning-making processes. This study investigated the processes by which adult women survivors of CSA, diagnosed with CPTSD and/or BPD, understand their experiences of abuse, interpret their psychiatric diagnoses, and integrate these distinct yet interconnected aspects of their lived experience. Semi-structured interviews were conducted with 19 adult women survivors of CSA, and the resulting data were analyzed employing interpretive description and reflexive thematic analysis. This analysis identified a central theme: making sense of the abuse and its consequences over time. This theme encompassed four subthemes: fragmented memory, containment, and silence; internal struggles of recognition and interpretation; familial validation and invalidation; and institutional responses that either connected or failed to connect trauma to its consequences. Across these domains, psychiatric diagnoses served as relational frameworks, with their meaning being negotiated across intrapsychic, familial, and institutional contexts. These findings propose a preliminary socioecological framework that extends Kelley et al.'s model of traumatic stress to the concept of recognition itself, herein conceptualized as relational and dynamically unfolding. Clinically, these findings underscore the importance of trauma-informed diagnostic communication that collaboratively explores possible links between current difficulties and abuse history while attending to safety, pacing, and readiness. They also highlight the importance of institutional practices that integrate trauma-related information across services and into clinical formulation.

### 5. Use of psychotropic medication among adolescents with borderline personality disorder: a 6-year descriptive follow-up study

- **Authors:** Lenskjold TU, Jørgensen MS, Bo S, Storebø OJ, Poulsen SB, Beck E, Kongerslev MT, Hastrup LH, Maagensen M, Simonsen E
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Nordic Journal of Psychiatry
- **DOI:** 10.1080/08039488.2026.2749574
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43013732/

**Abstract:**

INTRODUCTION: Psychotropic medications are widely used in the treatment of borderline personality disorder (BPD), despite limited supporting evidence. Characterizing the use of psychotropic medications in adolescents with BPD is a prerequisite for understanding the underlying factors that contribute to prescribing patterns, as well as for critically evaluating the clinical indications for such treatments. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to longitudinally assess psychotropic medication use in a sample of adolescents with BPD or BPD features over a six-year period.

METHODS: Medication data were obtained from 110 adolescents with BPD or BPD features from medical records at baseline and at follow-ups after one, two, and six years. Descriptive analyses were conducted to characterize prescription patterns over time.

RESULTS: Over the six-year follow-up period, the proportion of participants prescribed psychotropic medication remained high, increasing from 47% at baseline to 77% at the six-year follow-up, and polypharmacy was prevalent. Sertraline and quetiapine were most frequently prescribed, with quetiapine often used as pro re nata (PRN) medication.

CONCLUSIONS: Our results indicate that treatment with psychotropic medication for BPD is initiated during adolescence and sustained over time, with high prescription rates and polypharmacy. High levels of psychiatric comorbidity and variable courses of BPD may, among other factors, influence the observed prescribing patterns. Considering the limited evidence supporting their effectiveness, and the potential adverse effects, our findings emphasize the need for research into the factors driving the excessive use of psychotropic medications in BPD.

### 6. Meta-Analysis of Comorbidities Among Hysteriform Disorders: Is Borderline Personality Disorder a 'Hysterical Disorder'?

- **Authors:** Scotti-Muzzi E, Cunha D, Cheniaux E
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Personality and Mental Health
- **DOI:** 10.1080/15299732.2014.938214 (this DOI appears to be a recycled/mismatched identifier from an unrelated 2014 record — flagged, not corrected speculatively; see Classification notes)
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43016046/

**Abstract:**

Contemporary psychiatric classifications have fragmented the historical construct of hysteria into multiple diagnoses, including conversion disorder, somatic symptom disorder, dissociative disorders, depersonalization/derealization disorder, histrionic personality disorder and borderline personality disorder. However, substantial clinical overlap and high comorbidity rates challenge their classification as distinct diagnostic entities. This meta-analysis examined the prevalence of comorbidities among these conditions. Sixty-nine studies reporting prevalence data for at least one target disorder were included, with subgroup analyses by diagnostic criteria and meta-regression by publication year. Substantial cross-diagnostic comorbidity emerged, particularly involving borderline personality disorder, which showed strong bidirectional associations with dissociative and somatic symptom disorders. Heterogeneity was high but decreased in sensitivity analyses, and no temporal effects were detected. These findings suggest considerable overlap among hysteria-related conditions, with borderline personality disorder as a central diagnostic hub. Future classification systems should consider regrouping these conditions within a unified Hysteriform category, potentially reducing diagnostic inflation and misclassification.

### 7. Psychopathology and Suicidal Ideation Among Female Veterans Exposed to Military Sexual Trauma

- **Authors:** Lee SM, Tuchman FR, Coolidge FL
- **Year:** 2026
- **Journal:** Psychological Reports
- **DOI:** 10.1177/00332941261509602
- **PubMed URL (Google Scholar not used — see header note):** https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43024859/

**Abstract:**

Female veterans die by suicide at roughly twice the rate of female civilians, and military sexual trauma is a known risk factor. Little is known, however, about the relation between personality disorders and suicidal ideation in female veterans who have experienced military sexual trauma. This study examined personality disorder symptoms and other clinical conditions in relation to suicidal ideation. Participants were 92 female veterans who endorsed military sexual trauma and anonymously completed five measures assessing for suicidal ideation and psychopathology. Ordinal logistic regression models tested associations between suicidal ideation and (a) ten personality disorder symptom scales and (b) post-traumatic stress disorder symptoms, depression, anxiety, and substance use disorders, tested both together and separately. Nearly half of participants (48.9%) reported suicidal ideation at the highest risk level. Borderline personality disorder symptoms were associated with more severe suicidal ideation (OR = 1.26, 95% CI [1.14, 1.41]); the other nine personality disorder symptom scales were not significantly associated with suicidal ideation. Post-traumatic stress disorder symptoms, depression, and anxiety were each associated with suicidal ideation in separate models, but none remained significant when entered together with alcohol and drug use disorders. Substance use disorders were not associated with suicidal ideation in either set of models. Borderline features may be an informative marker of risk for suicidal ideation among female veterans with military sexual trauma. However, these findings require replication in larger samples, and causality ought to be explored in longitudinal designs.

**Abstract:**

The pivotal role of the quality of the parent-child relationship in the child's cognitive, emotional, and social development is widely acknowledged in contemporary academic discourse. Despite the fact that the operation of these relational dynamics remains to be fully elucidated, research has already indicated the central role of mentalization and parental reflective functioning in these processes. The present literature review focuses primarily on the deficit of reflective functioning, emotion regulation and psychological availability among mothers with borderline personality disorder, and its potential connections with the mother-child interaction, the quality and transmission of attachment and the child's development. Furthermore, the analysis underlines the potential symptoms and disorders emerging in early childhood that are related to dysfunctional mother-child interactions. In this discussion, we will examine the various methods of prevention and intervention, with a particular focus on integrated parent-infant consultation. The objective of this method is to enhance the mother-child relationship and, by extension, to improve reflective functioning. The study calls for the further development of psychodynamic and mentalization-based interventions, as well as the broadening of possible preventive methods.
