# BPD Literature Screening and Classification Decision Rules

## Purpose

This document defines the rules for a weekly literature-monitoring task focused on **borderline personality disorder (BPD)**. The task should identify newly published literature and classify papers according to whether they involve:

1. Psychometrics / measurement
2. Psychotherapy
3. Pharmacotherapy
4. Other clinical interventions
5. Assessment / diagnosis
6. Adolescent BPD
7. Epidemiology / phenomenology
8. Neurobiology / neuroscience
9. Comorbidity
10. Qualitative / lived-experience research
11. Reviews / meta-analyses
12. Other BPD-relevant research
13. Mentalization / Reflective Functioning
14. Psychodynamic

The classifier should prioritize **substantive relevance** over simple keyword matching. A paper should not be classified into a category merely because the relevant terminology appears somewhere in the abstract.

---

# 1. General Screening Rule

For every newly identified paper, determine:

* Is BPD/borderline personality disorder a substantive focus of the paper?
* What is the paper primarily studying?
* Does it involve psychometric evaluation?
* Does it evaluate psychotherapy?
* Does it evaluate another treatment/intervention?
* Does it substantially concern adolescents or adolescent development?
* What additional categories apply?

Use the **title, abstract, keywords, reported sample characteristics, and available full-text information** when possible.

If only an abstract is available, make the classification based on the available evidence and indicate uncertainty when appropriate.

## BPD terminology to recognize

The search/classification system should recognize:

* borderline personality disorder
* borderline personality
* BPD
* borderline patients
* patients with borderline personality disorder
* borderline pathology
* borderline features
* borderline traits
* borderline symptoms
* borderline personality pathology

Be cautious with the abbreviation **BPD**, because it can have meanings unrelated to borderline personality disorder. Confirm the context before classifying a paper.

# 2. Psychometrics Classification

## Definition

Classify a paper as **Psychometrics = Yes** when the paper's substantive purpose includes developing, evaluating, validating, adapting, or examining the measurement properties of a psychological measure relevant to BPD or BPD-related constructs.

Psychometric research may concern:

* a BPD diagnostic/assessment instrument;
* a BPD symptom scale;
* a measure of borderline traits;
* a measure of a construct central to BPD;
* a measure specifically intended for use with people with BPD.

# 4. Psychometric Decision Rules

## Classify as "Yes — High Confidence" when:

The paper has BPD/BPD-related measurement as a substantive objective AND evaluates one or more measurement properties.

Examples:

* "Confirmatory factor analysis of the Borderline Symptom List"
* "Validation of the Spanish version of the MSI-BPD"
* "Measurement invariance of a borderline personality disorder scale"
* "Psychometric properties of a measure of borderline personality symptoms"
* "Development and validation of a brief BPD screening instrument"

## Classify as "Possible" when:

Psychometric methods are present but it is unclear whether measurement evaluation is a substantive purpose.

Examples:

* A clinical BPD study reports Cronbach's alpha for several outcome measures.
* A BPD treatment trial performs a CFA as a secondary analysis.
* A paper uses multiple validated scales but does not primarily investigate their psychometric properties.

## Do NOT classify as Psychometrics merely because:

* a questionnaire is used;
* Cronbach's alpha is reported;
* reliability statistics appear incidentally;
* factor analysis is used for a secondary purpose unrelated to evaluating a BPD measure;
* BPD patients happen to participate in a study validating a measure of an unrelated construct;
* the paper uses a validated BPD measure solely as an outcome variable.

### Key principle

> Distinguish **"the paper evaluates a measure"** from **"the paper uses a measure."**

The former qualifies as psychometric research; the latter generally does not.

# 8. Psychotherapy Decision Rules

## Classify as "Yes — High Confidence" when:

The paper evaluates a named psychotherapy or psychological intervention for BPD.

Examples:

* "Dialectical behavior therapy for borderline personality disorder: a randomized trial"
* "Mentalization-based treatment in adolescents with borderline personality disorder"
* "Schema therapy versus transference-focused psychotherapy for BPD"
* "Treatment outcomes following general psychiatric management for borderline personality disorder"

## Classify as "Possible" when:

The paper describes treatment/intervention research but the nature of the intervention is unclear from the available information.

Examples:

* "A pilot intervention for emotional dysregulation in patients with BPD"
* "Treatment outcomes in a specialized BPD clinic"

If the intervention is clearly pharmacological, it should instead be classified as **Pharmacotherapy**.

## Do NOT classify as Psychotherapy merely because:

* psychotherapy is mentioned in the introduction;
* psychotherapy is listed as a recommended treatment;
* participants have previously received psychotherapy;
* psychotherapy is one element of standard care but is not being evaluated;
* a medication study mentions psychotherapy as an alternative;
* a paper discusses psychotherapy theoretically without studying an intervention.

### Key principle

> Distinguish **"the paper evaluates psychotherapy"** from **"the paper mentions psychotherapy."**

# 9. Adolescent BPD Classification

## Definition

**Adolescent BPD** should be a separate flag from Psychometrics, Psychotherapy, and the other categories.

This allows the weekly literature search to identify, for example:

* psychometric studies of BPD measures in adolescents;
* psychotherapy trials for adolescents with BPD;
* developmental studies of BPD during adolescence;
* adolescent BPD epidemiology;
* adolescent BPD neurobiology;

A paper can therefore have:

> Psychometrics = Yes
> Psychotherapy = No
> Adolescent BPD = Yes

# 10. Adolescent BPD Decision Rules

Use the following classification:

### "Yes — Adolescent focus"

Use when adolescents are the primary population being studied.

Examples:

* "Borderline personality disorder in adolescents"
* "Validation of the Borderline Symptom List in adolescents"
* "Dialectical behavior therapy for adolescents with BPD"
* "Developmental trajectories of borderline symptoms from ages 13 to 17"

### "Yes — Includes adolescents"

Use when adolescents are included in a mixed-age sample and represent a meaningful part of the population.

Example:

* "BPD in young people aged 16–30"

### "Possible"

Use when the population may include adolescents but the available information is insufficient to establish this confidently.

### "No"

Use when the study is clearly adult-only.

For example:

* sample ages 25–50;
* adult psychiatric outpatients;
* participants explicitly described as adults with no adolescent subgroup.

---

# 11. Age-Based Decision Rules

Whenever available, record the **actual reported age range**.

Do not rely solely on labels such as "young people" or "young adults."

For example:

> Adolescent BPD: Yes — Adolescent focus
> Age range: 13–17

or:

> Adolescent BPD: Yes — Includes adolescents
> Age range: 16–35

or:

> Adolescent BPD: Possible
> Age range: Not reported in abstract

### Young adults

Treat **young adult/emerging adult** as potentially overlapping with adolescence, but do not automatically classify these papers as adolescent BPD.

Use **Possible** unless the reported age range clearly includes adolescents.

For example:

* Ages 18–25 → No adolescent flag
* Ages 16–25 → Yes — Includes adolescents
* "Young adults" with no ages → Possible

# Mentalization and Reflective Functioning Classification

## Definition

Classify a paper as **Mentalization & Reflective Functioning = Yes**
when mentalization and/or reflective functioning (RF) is a substantive
part of the paper's **main analyses** — RF is measured, or tested as a
predictor, mediator, moderator, or outcome, or mentalizing capacity is
itself the central theoretical or empirical focus.

This is a separate flag from Psychotherapy, the same way Adolescent BPD
is separate from Psychotherapy and Psychometrics (§9). A paper can
deliver Mentalization-Based Treatment (MBT) without analyzing
mentalization/RF as a variable, and a paper can analyze RF without
delivering MBT (e.g., an observational study of parental reflective
functioning and adolescent BPD symptoms).

## Terminology to recognize

* mentalization
* mentalizing
* reflective functioning
* RF
* reflective function
* reflective functioning scale / RFS
* RFQ / Reflective Functioning Questionnaire
* mentalizing capacity
* epistemic trust
* hypermentalizing
* hypomentalizing
* mind-mindedness
* parental reflective functioning / PRF
* Certainty about Mental States Questionnaire / CAMSQ
* theory of mind (when used in a mentalizing/RF research context)
* Movie for the Assessment of Social Cognition / MASC (when used to
  assess mentalizing)
* mentalization-based treatment / MBT (evidence of topical adjacency —
  see caution below; delivering MBT alone is not sufficient)

## Decision rules

### Classify as "Yes — High Confidence" when:

RF or mentalization is directly measured, analyzed, or is the central
theoretical construct under study.

Examples:

* "Reflective functioning as a mediator of treatment outcome in
  borderline personality disorder"
* "Parental reflective functioning and adolescent BPD symptoms: a
  longitudinal study"
* "Hypermentalizing and social cognition in borderline personality
  disorder"

### Classify as "Possible" when:

Mentalization is discussed conceptually, or MBT is delivered/evaluated,
but RF/mentalizing itself is not directly measured or analyzed as a
primary variable.

Example:

* A psychotherapy-outcome study of MBT that references mentalization
  theory in its introduction but reports only symptom-outcome measures.

### Do NOT classify as Yes merely because:

* the paper describes or delivers MBT without measuring RF/mentalization
  directly (that's Psychotherapy — it may also be Mentalization & RF, but
  only if RF/mentalizing is itself analyzed);
* "mentalization" appears once, in the introduction or discussion, as
  theoretical framing rather than part of the actual analysis;
* "theory of mind" is mentioned in an unrelated context.

### Key principle

> Distinguish **"the paper analyzes mentalization or reflective
> functioning"** from **"the paper delivers a mentalization-based
> treatment"** or **"the paper mentions mentalization theoretically."**
> A paper can be both Psychotherapy = Yes (MBT) and Mentalization & RF =
> Yes if RF is also measured or analyzed as an outcome — but delivering
> MBT alone does not automatically trigger Mentalization & RF = Yes.

# Psychodynamic Classification

## Definition

Classify a paper as **Psychodynamic = Yes** when it substantively
engages psychodynamic or psychoanalytic theory or practice regarding
BPD — either **theoretical/conceptual** (e.g., object relations theory,
ego psychology, self psychology, psychoanalytic case formulation,
borderline personality organization) or **clinical/empirical**
(delivering, evaluating, or studying a named psychodynamic/
psychoanalytic therapy).

This is a separate flag, the same way Mentalization & Reflective
Functioning and Adolescent BPD are separate flags — a paper can be
Psychodynamic = Yes without being Psychotherapy = Yes (a purely
theoretical object-relations paper with no treatment-outcome component),
and a paper can be both (a transference-focused psychotherapy RCT is
Psychotherapy = Yes *and* Psychodynamic = Yes).

Mentalization & Reflective Functioning and Psychodynamic will often
co-occur — psychoanalytic theory is Fonagy's mentalization framework's
historical foundation — but they are not the same flag. A paper can be
Psychodynamic = Yes without engaging RF/mentalizing specifically (e.g.,
a paper purely about splitting or transference), and Mentalization & RF
= Yes without a psychodynamic frame (e.g., a cognitive-neuroscience
theory-of-mind study with no psychoanalytic theoretical grounding).

## Terminology to recognize

* psychodynamic
* psychoanalytic / psychoanalysis
* object relations / object relations theory
* ego psychology
* self psychology
* transference
* countertransference
* defense mechanism(s)
* splitting
* projective identification
* primitive defenses
* borderline personality organization (Kernberg's term — related to but
  not identical to the DSM/ICD "borderline personality disorder"
  diagnosis; flag the distinction rather than silently treating them as
  interchangeable, see Do NOT classify below)
* unconscious conflict
* psychodynamic therapy
* psychoanalytic therapy / psychoanalytic psychotherapy
* transference-focused psychotherapy / TFP
* supportive-expressive therapy
* brief psychodynamic therapy
* psychodynamic case formulation
* interpretive technique / interpretation (clinical-technique sense)

Also recognize theorist names as topical signals when discussed in a
psychoanalytic-theory context: Kernberg, Kohut, Klein, Winnicott.
(Fonagy is a special case — his mentalization work is psychoanalytically
rooted, but a Fonagy-authored/cited paper should be evaluated on its own
content against *both* this category and Mentalization & RF, not
auto-flagged for either based on the name alone.)

## Decision rules

### Classify as "Yes — High Confidence" when:

The paper's central content is psychodynamic/psychoanalytic theory or
technique, or it delivers/evaluates a named psychodynamic/psychoanalytic
therapy for BPD.

Examples:

* "Recent Developments in Object Relations Theory" (theoretical chapter
  discussing borderline personality organization)
* "Transference-focused psychotherapy versus schema therapy for BPD: a
  randomized trial"
* "A psychoanalytic differentiation between complex PTSD and borderline
  personality disorder"
* "Splitting and projective identification in borderline patients: a
  case series"

### Classify as "Possible" when:

Psychodynamic concepts appear as one lens among several, or a
personality-pathology theory paper touches psychoanalytic ideas without
being centrally organized around them.

### Do NOT classify as Yes merely because:

* "personality" or general trait/theory language is used without
  psychoanalytic framing;
* a therapy-outcome paper uses a word like "insight" once, without
  psychodynamic technique or theory being part of the actual content;
* "borderline personality organization" appears — confirm the paper is
  actually working within Kernberg's psychodynamic structural framework,
  not just using the phrase loosely as a synonym for the DSM/ICD
  diagnosis.

### Key principle

> Distinguish **"the paper is substantively organized around
> psychodynamic/psychoanalytic theory or technique"** from **"the paper
> mentions a psychodynamic concept in passing."** A paper can be both
> Psychotherapy = Yes and Psychodynamic = Yes (e.g., a TFP outcome
> trial), Psychodynamic = Yes alone (a purely theoretical paper), or
> Psychotherapy = Yes without Psychodynamic (e.g., a DBT trial has
> nothing psychodynamic about it).

# 18. Comorbidity

Use this category when the primary research question concerns BPD in relation to another disorder or clinical condition.

Examples:

* BPD and PTSD

* BPD and substance use
* BPD and bipolar disorder

Comorbidity may be a secondary category rather than the primary category.

### Bipolar spectrum comorbidity — highlighted

BPD's overlap and differential diagnosis with bipolar spectrum disorders
(**Bipolar I and Bipolar II, both count**) is common enough, and
clinically significant enough (misdiagnosis risk runs in both
directions), that it should be flagged explicitly rather than left as
one bullet buried in the general comorbidity list above.

Recognize:

* bipolar disorder
* bipolar I disorder
* bipolar II disorder
* BD-I / BD-II
* bipolar spectrum
* cyclothymia / cyclothymic disorder
* manic episode
* hypomanic episode
* mixed features (mood episode specifier)

When a paper substantively concerns BPD in relation to any bipolar
spectrum disorder, record it in the dedicated `Comorbid — Bipolar
spectrum (I/II)` field in the per-paper output (§23) — don't only fold
it into a generic "Other categories" note where it's easy to miss when
scanning the week's output.

# Notable Authors & Affiliations — Highlighted

This is a cross-cutting flag, independent of the topical categories
above. Regardless of a paper's substantive-relevance screening result or
category classification, always flag it if any of the following appear
as an author, or if the paper is affiliated with the Personality
Disorders Institute (PDI) at Weill Cornell Medical College:

* Peter Fonagy
* Frank Yeomans
* Otto Kernberg
* Kenneth Levy
* John Clarkin
* Personality Disorders Institute, Weill Cornell (any author affiliation)

These are core figures behind mentalization-based treatment (Fonagy) and
transference-focused psychotherapy / the psychodynamic object-relations
model of BPD (Kernberg, Yeomans, Clarkin, Levy) — closely tied to the
Psychodynamic and Mentalization & Reflective Functioning categories
above, and based at or historically associated with Weill Cornell's PDI.

## Matching guidance

* Match on full name where possible. Scholar-style abbreviated first
  names are acceptable matches (e.g., "Otto F Kernberg," "OF Kernberg,"
  and "O Kernberg" all match "Otto Kernberg").
* A bare surname match alone — "Levy" or "Clarkin" are common surnames —
  without a matching first initial, or without independent supporting
  evidence (co-authors from the same research group, a PDI/Weill Cornell
  affiliation, clear topic fit with this person's known work), is not
  sufficient for a confirmed match. Mark it **Possible** and say what's
  uncertain, rather than asserting a confirmed match on a surname alone.
* This flag does **not** depend on the substantive-relevance screening
  used elsewhere in this document. A paper by one of these authors
  should be surfaced even if it would otherwise be borderline-relevance
  or excluded on topical grounds — note in Classification notes that it
  was kept (or specially flagged) specifically because of the
  author/affiliation match, not because it independently cleared the
  usual relevance bar.

## Output requirements

Add to each paper's classification block:

```text
Notable author/affiliation match:
  Yes / No / Possible
  Matched on: [name(s) and/or affiliation, or "n/a"]
```

Add a dedicated subsection near the top of the weekly aggregate summary
(§24), immediately after "Most relevant new papers":

```text
NOTABLE AUTHORS/AFFILIATIONS FLAGGED THIS WEEK

1. [Title] — matched: [name or affiliation]
2. [Title] — matched: [name or affiliation]
```

If none matched, state that explicitly (`None this week`) rather than
omitting the subsection — a real run should always show whether this
check ran, not just silently skip it in a quiet week.

# 21. Multiple-Category Classification

Categories are **not mutually exclusive**.

A paper may legitimately receive multiple classifications.

### Example A

"Psychometric evaluation of the Borderline Symptom List in a clinical sample"

* Psychometrics: Yes — High confidence
* Psychotherapy: No
* Adolescent BPD: No/depends on sample
* Assessment/Diagnosis: Yes
* Primary category: Psychometrics

### Example B

"Dialectical behavior therapy for borderline personality disorder: a randomized controlled trial"

* Psychometrics: No
* Psychotherapy: Yes — High confidence
* Adolescent BPD: No/depends on sample
* Intervention: Yes
* Primary category: Psychotherapy

### Example C

"Validation of a BPD screening instrument in adolescents"

* Psychometrics: Yes — High confidence
* Assessment/Diagnosis: Yes
* Adolescent BPD: Yes — Adolescent focus
* Primary category: Psychometrics

### Example D

"Dialectical behavior therapy for adolescents with borderline personality disorder"

# 22. Confidence Levels

Every category should receive a confidence level:

* **High** — The title/abstract clearly establishes that the category is a substantive focus.
* **Moderate** — Evidence supports the category, but the available information is incomplete or somewhat ambiguous.
* **Low/Possible** — There is a plausible connection, but insufficient evidence for a confident classification.
* **No** — The paper clearly does not meet the category definition.

Do not convert weak keyword evidence into a high-confidence classification.

# 26. False-Positive Control

Before including a paper in the high-priority results, ask:

1. Is BPD actually a substantive focus?
2. Is the category a substantive focus rather than a passing mention?
3. If classifying as Psychometrics, is a measure actually being evaluated?
4. If classifying as Psychotherapy, is psychotherapy actually being delivered, evaluated, compared, or studied?
5. If classifying as Adolescent BPD, does the sample actually include adolescents or is adolescence a substantive developmental focus?
6. Could the keyword be referring to something else?
7. Is the classification supported by the title/abstract rather than inferred solely from a keyword?
8. Is the age range known, and if so, what does it imply about the population?

If the answer is uncertain, use **Possible/Moderate confidence** rather than forcing a Yes.

# 27. Primary Category Rule

When several categories apply, identify a **Primary Category** based on the paper's central research question.

Use this hierarchy only as a tie-breaker:

1. Psychometrics / measurement
2. Psychotherapy
3. Pharmacotherapy
4. Other intervention
5. Assessment / diagnosis
6. Adolescent BPD / developmental research
7. Mentalization & Reflective Functioning
8. Psychodynamic
9. Epidemiology / phenomenology
10. Neurobiology / neuroscience
11. Comorbidity
12. Qualitative / lived experience
13. Other

This hierarchy should **not** override the actual purpose of the paper.

For example, a psychotherapy RCT that happens to use several validated scales remains primarily a **Psychotherapy** paper, not a psychometrics paper.

Similarly, an adolescent psychotherapy trial should remain primarily a **Psychotherapy** paper while also receiving:

> Adolescent BPD = Yes — Adolescent focus

# 28. Fundamental Classification Principles

The classifier should use **semantic/contextual evidence rather than literal keyword matching**.

### Psychometrics

> **"Is this paper substantially about developing, validating, adapting, or evaluating the measurement properties of a BPD/BPD-relevant measure?"**

### Psychotherapy

> **"Is this paper substantially about delivering, evaluating, comparing, developing, or understanding a psychotherapy/psychological treatment for BPD?"**

### Adolescent BPD

> **"Does this paper substantially concern BPD, borderline pathology, or BPD-related development, assessment, or treatment in adolescents, or does its sample meaningfully include adolescents?"**

### Mentalization & Reflective Functioning

> **"Does this paper substantially measure, analyze, or theoretically center mentalization or reflective functioning as part of its main analyses — not merely deliver a mentalization-based treatment or mention the concept in passing?"**

### Psychodynamic

> **"Is this paper substantively organized around psychodynamic or psychoanalytic theory or technique — not merely using a term like 'borderline personality organization' or 'insight' in passing?"**

The classifier should distinguish:

**Mention/use of a construct or instrument ≠ substantive study of that construct or instrument.**

**Mention of psychotherapy ≠ psychotherapy study.**

**Delivering mentalization-based treatment ≠ analyzing mentalization or
reflective functioning.**

**Using the phrase "borderline personality organization" ≠ automatically
a substantive psychodynamic paper — confirm it's actually working within
that theoretical framework.**

**Use of the word "young" ≠ automatically an adolescent sample.**

When possible, verify the **actual participant age range**.

# 29. Final Output Philosophy

The goal of this system is not simply to find papers containing particular words.

The goal is to identify **new BPD literature that is meaningfully useful for research monitoring**, while making it easy to filter for:

* psychometric/measurement research;
* psychotherapy research;
* adolescent BPD research;
* pharmacological research;
* assessment/diagnostic research;
* and other major BPD research domains.

When uncertain, preserve the paper and mark the classification as **Possible** rather than silently excluding potentially relevant literature.
